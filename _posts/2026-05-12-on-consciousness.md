This is a post that collects my ongoing quest to form my own opinion on the nature of consciousness. I plan to update this regularly as I develop deeper opinions on the matter.

The modern stance is that consciousness is not a problem that needs solving. Neuroscience has increasingly shown that the mind and the brain are tightly bound and that there is no need to posit a "soul" that explains features of our conscious experience. Because of this trend, we can know that the brain and consciousness are really just two terms for the same thing depending on what perspective we are taking.

In one sense, I find myself largely agreeing with this position. I don't find it necessary to posit extra meta-physical entities like the soul. My qualm is that this position fails to capture the most interesting part of explaining consciousness. This is the fact that we have two parallel discourses about the mind that are fundamentally incompatible. On one hand, we have the scientific discourse and on the other is phenominalogical experience. Although we can show a one-to-one correspondence between these two discourses. The presumptive metaphysic underlying each are in opposition to eachother. For example, the scientific discourse generally presumes some sort of physicalist worldview where reality is nothing but quantum fields in complex configurations. In opposition, the phenominological discourse uses terms like thoughts, beliefs, and experiences. These are in my opinion realist terms for our mental lives. 

The modern thinker would say that that we can easily map each facet of our inner lives to a particular pattern in the brain. We can show how these patterns function and interact with eachother and thereby we have shown that the mental experiences are nothing but patterns. What I find troubling is that this is a lossy conversion. 

Although structure is preserved, the actual contents and experiences are truely lost and unaccounted for in the scientific perspective. Why is the red wavelength feel like red? The scientific account can only explain the structure of our color experiences but not anything more. We must just assume that we have this subjective experience ocurring on the inside. 

My proposal is that we acknowledge that each of these is a limited perspective on the true nature of reality and that we need better metaphysical language that can encompass both the third and first person descriptions.

An example of this project would be the neutral monism of Betrand Russell. He acknowledges the legitimacy of both perspectives while also recognizing a need to update our metaphysic to encompass them both.
